TRAINING DOCUMENT ONLY: Northstar Air is a fictional airline. All fees, dimensions other than the preserved personal-item rule, deadlines, eligibility rules, and procedures below are invented for classroom exercises. Do not use this policy for real travel decisions.

1. Purpose and Scope
This policy establishes Northstar Air rules for passenger carry-on items, checked baggage, special items, travel with children and pets, and selected ticket-change and refund situations. It is intended to provide a single, consistent reference for customer-service scenarios and for training retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. The rules apply to Northstar Air-operated domestic flights within the fictional network. Codeshare flights and partner-operated services are outside the scope of this document unless specifically identified in a booking confirmation.

The policy is organized into numbered sections so readers can locate a rule without relying on surrounding paragraphs. When a customer asks about baggage, the applicable fare type, item dimensions, and storage location should be checked together. A general allowance does not override a fare-specific exception. Staff should explain both the permission and the conditions that limit it. Examples are illustrations of the written rules and do not create additional allowances.

For this training exercise, the terms carry-on bag, personal item, and checked bag are distinct. A carry-on is placed in an overhead bin, a personal item is placed under the seat, and a checked bag travels in the aircraft hold. Passengers should review both their fare conditions and the relevant item category before arriving at the airport. The airline may require gate checking when overhead space is unavailable, even when the passenger is otherwise eligible for a carry-on.
2. Carry-On Baggage and Personal Items
2.1 Standard Carry-On Allowance
Passengers traveling on Standard, Flex, or Premium fares may bring one carry-on bag and one personal item into the cabin, subject to space and safety restrictions. The carry-on bag must fit in the overhead bin. The personal item must fit completely under the seat in front of the passenger.

Example rule: A backpack may qualify as the personal item if it fits within the personal-item dimensions and can be stowed fully under the seat. A passenger carrying both a roller suitcase and a backpack should identify which is the overhead-bin carry-on and which is the under-seat personal item. An item cannot be counted as a personal item merely because its owner calls it a backpack. Its actual dimensions and storage requirements determine the classification.

For the fictional classroom scenario, the standard overhead-bin carry-on limit is 22 × 14 × 9 inches, including wheels and handles. An eligible carry-on that exceeds this limit must be checked or otherwise handled under the oversized-item procedures. Cabin crew may request that an otherwise compliant carry-on be gate checked for operational reasons. Gate checking due to limited bin space does not change the passenger's underlying fare allowance.

Passengers should keep medications, identification, travel documents, fragile electronics, and other essential items in a bag they can retain when possible. If a carry-on is gate checked, these items should be removed before handoff. A small shopping bag, camera case, or laptop sleeve may count toward the existing item allowance rather than creating a third complimentary bag. The passenger's entitlement remains one carry-on and one personal item on the eligible fares described above.
2.2 Personal Item Definition
A personal item may include a backpack, purse, briefcase, laptop bag, or similarly sized item. Maximum personal-item dimensions are 18 × 14 × 8 inches, including handles and wheels. A backpack that exceeds these dimensions is treated as a carry-on bag rather than a personal item.

A personal item must fit completely under the seat in front of the passenger. A soft-sided backpack that compresses within the stated dimensions may qualify, provided it can be safely stowed. Straps, external pockets, and attached accessories count toward the overall dimensions. Items may not block the aisle, occupy the footwell beyond the under-seat storage area, or interfere with emergency evacuation.

The personal-item category describes the item’s size and storage location, not the purpose for which it is used. A work backpack and a school backpack are evaluated using the same rules. If a traveler brings two backpacks, each is evaluated separately; one may be the carry-on if the fare permits a carry-on, but two personal items are not automatically allowed. A traveler should not assume that a laptop bag is exempt from the item count simply because it contains a computer.
2.3 Basic Saver Fare Exception
Basic Saver passengers are limited to one personal item and do not receive a standard carry-on allowance. A full-size carry-on is permitted only with eligible status. This exception takes precedence over the general Standard, Flex, and Premium allowance.

For this synthetic exercise, eligible status means a qualifying Northstar Air Priority Traveler benefit explicitly shown on the reservation. The benefit must be active for the flight in question. Buying a Basic Saver ticket does not by itself grant a carry-on, and paying for a checked bag does not convert that checked bag into a cabin allowance. A backpack within the personal-item limit can be carried under the seat on Basic Saver; a separate roller carry-on is not included unless the eligible-status exception applies.

When explaining this rule, staff should identify the fare first, then distinguish a backpack used as a personal item from a backpack large enough to count as a carry-on. A traveler with a Basic Saver ticket and two cabin-sized items may need to check one item or change to an eligible fare. Any optional purchase or fare change should be evaluated under the conditions shown in the reservation rather than assumed to be available in every case.
2.4 Cabin Storage and Boarding Procedures
All cabin items must be safely stored for taxi, takeoff, and landing. Overhead-bin space is shared and is not guaranteed to be adjacent to a passenger's seat. Crew instructions take priority when an item must be repositioned or gate checked. Passengers may be asked to remove loose straps, secure zippers, or separate fragile contents before placing a bag in an overhead bin.

A passenger should not place a personal item in the overhead bin when the under-seat area is available and the crew requests that space be reserved for carry-on luggage. Exit-row seating may require different storage arrangements during critical phases of flight. These operational rules do not increase the number of bags permitted by a fare. A bag that cannot be safely accommodated in the cabin must travel under the applicable checked-baggage or gate-check process.
3. Checked Baggage
3.1 Standard Checked-Bag Limits
For the fictional Northstar Air training scenario, a standard checked bag may weigh up to 50 pounds and have a maximum combined length, width, and height of 62 linear inches. Handles and wheels count toward measured dimensions. Bags exceeding either standard threshold are subject to the oversized or overweight rules described below. These figures are synthetic classroom assumptions, not real airline guidance.

A checked bag must be securely closed and tagged to the passenger's final Northstar Air destination on the itinerary. The passenger should remove old routing labels, attach identifying contact information, and retain the claim receipt. Northstar Air may decline an item that is unsafe to transport, inadequately packed, or otherwise restricted. A checked-bag allowance is separate from the cabin carry-on and personal-item allowance.
3.2 Checked-Bag Fees and Fare Differences
In this fictional scenario, Standard and Basic Saver fares have a $35 first checked-bag fee and a $45 second checked-bag fee when purchased before airport check-in. Flex fares include one standard checked bag, and Premium fares include two standard checked bags. Additional bags are assessed according to the fee schedule shown in the booking record. These example fees are included to create realistic retrieval distractors for the lab.

A traveler should not confuse a free checked bag with permission to bring an additional carry-on into the cabin. Checked-bag benefits do not override Basic Saver carry-on restrictions. When a customer asks about a backpack and a carry-on, the relevant answer comes primarily from Section 2, not from this checked-baggage fee section.
3.3 Overweight, Oversized, and Additional Bags
For training purposes, bags weighing 51–70 pounds incur a fictional $75 overweight fee, and bags measuring 63–80 linear inches incur a fictional $100 oversized fee. Fees may accumulate when a bag exceeds both limits. Items beyond 70 pounds or 80 linear inches require separate approval and may be refused for transport on certain aircraft.

Passengers are responsible for packing checked items so they can withstand ordinary baggage handling. Fragile objects should be protected, and valuables should be carried in the cabin when permitted. The baggage desk may require repacking if a suitcase cannot be safely closed. Charges, acceptance decisions, and delivery expectations should be documented in the passenger's record.
3.4 Delayed, Lost, and Damaged Baggage
A passenger who does not receive a checked bag at the baggage carousel should report the issue before leaving the airport when practical. The fictional airline creates a baggage case number and requests the bag-tag receipt, itinerary, delivery address, and description of the bag. Updates are provided through the contact method selected in the case record.

Damage claims should describe the condition of the bag and include photographs when available. Ordinary cosmetic wear is evaluated differently from functional damage such as broken wheels or a damaged frame. The baggage support team reviews each case under the fictional carrier's claims process. A delayed checked bag is not the same as a cabin bag voluntarily gate checked because of overhead-bin space limitations.
4. Traveling with Children
4.1 Strollers and Child Restraint Systems
Northstar Air permits one collapsible stroller per traveling child to be checked at the ticket counter or gate without an additional handling charge in this fictional scenario. A stroller presented at the gate is tagged before boarding and may be returned at the arrival gate or baggage claim depending on airport operations. Parents should remove loose toys, bags, and accessories before handoff.

An approved child restraint system may be used in a purchased seat when it fits the aircraft seat and meets the fictional carrier's acceptance rules. The crew determines the permitted seating position. A child restraint system is not automatically treated as a standard carry-on bag when it is being used for an eligible child passenger. Families should confirm seating and equipment requirements before departure.
4.2 Infant Supplies and Family Boarding
A small diaper bag containing essential infant supplies may be carried in addition to the adult's normal personal item in this fictional training scenario when an infant is listed on the reservation. This special allowance is limited to essential infant care supplies and should not be used as an extra general-purpose suitcase. Items must still fit in approved storage locations.

Families traveling with young children may be invited to board during a designated family-boarding period. Early boarding does not guarantee overhead-bin space and does not change the fare's standard carry-on allowance. Questions about a backpack and a roller bag for an adult passenger should be answered using Section 2 unless a specific infant-related exception applies.
5. Special Items and Accessibility
5.1 Mobility and Medical Equipment
Necessary mobility aids and medically required assistive devices are handled separately from ordinary leisure baggage in this fictional policy. Passengers should notify Northstar Air when an item requires advance coordination or cannot fit in the cabin. The airport team may request details about size, battery type, handling requirements, and the assistance needed during boarding or arrival.

Essential medical supplies should be packed for easy inspection and safe access. Staff should avoid treating a medically necessary device as an ordinary extra personal item without reviewing the applicable accommodation procedure. When a device must be placed in the aircraft hold, the passenger should receive information about collection and transfer arrangements.
5.2 Sports and Musical Equipment
Sports equipment and musical instruments are assessed according to size, weight, packaging, and available aircraft space. A compact instrument may travel as the permitted carry-on if it fits the cabin limits and can be safely stowed. Larger instruments may require a purchased seat or checked handling, subject to advance approval in the fictional scenario.

Sports bags exceeding the standard checked-baggage limits may incur oversized or overweight charges. Hard cases are recommended for fragile equipment. Approval to transport special equipment does not automatically provide an extra cabin bag in addition to the passenger's normal fare allowance.
6. Pets in the Cabin
An in-cabin pet carrier counts as the passenger's personal item. A passenger traveling with a pet may still bring one carry-on bag if the fare otherwise includes a carry-on allowance.

For this fictional scenario, the pet must remain inside an approved ventilated carrier that fits under the seat throughout the flight. The carrier should be leak-resistant, secure, and large enough for the animal to rest comfortably. The reservation must identify the pet before travel, and the airline may limit the number of cabin pets on a particular flight. These additional handling requirements are synthetic training examples.

Because the pet carrier uses the personal-item allowance, a passenger should not assume that a separate backpack is also permitted as another personal item. A traveler with a pet, a roller suitcase, and a backpack may need to check or consolidate one of the bags. The applicable fare still determines whether the traveler may bring a separate overhead-bin carry-on.
7. Ticket Changes, Cancellations, and Refunds
7.1 Voluntary Changes
In this fictional scenario, Standard and Flex passengers may request changes before scheduled departure, subject to seat availability and any fare difference. Basic Saver changes are restricted and may require an eligible waiver. Premium fares may have more flexible change conditions, but a confirmed seat on a replacement flight is not guaranteed until the change is completed.

Changing a ticket can alter the baggage benefits associated with the fare. A passenger who changes from Flex to Basic Saver should review the new fare's carry-on and checked-bag rules. Staff should not answer baggage questions solely from a prior itinerary if the passenger's current booking shows a different fare type.
7.2 Cancellations and Travel Credits
A passenger who cancels a qualifying ticket before departure may receive a refund or travel credit depending on the fare rules shown in the reservation. For classroom purposes, unused Standard tickets may receive a fictional travel credit valid for 12 months from the original issue date, while eligible Flex refunds return to the original form of payment. These conditions are invented to support training comparisons.

Travel credits cannot be assumed to cover optional baggage charges unless the booking system explicitly permits it. Refund eligibility for a canceled flight is distinct from the baggage allowance on a flight that the passenger actually takes. Agents should confirm the reservation status before discussing any available remedy.
7.3 Airline-Initiated Disruptions
When Northstar Air cancels a flight or makes a major schedule change in this synthetic scenario, affected passengers may be offered rebooking or another remedy according to the fictional disruption policy. A rebooked passenger should receive an updated itinerary and baggage entitlement summary. Any special accommodation for children, pets, or mobility equipment should be carried forward when feasible.

An operational disruption does not change the basic definition of a personal item. A backpack remains subject to the 18 × 14 × 8 inch personal-item limit unless a separately documented exception applies. For a direct carry-on-and-backpack question, Section 2 remains the primary source even when a passenger has recently been rebooked.
8. Airport Check-In, Security, and Boarding
8.1 Check-In and Bag Drop
Passengers should complete check-in and present checked baggage before the deadline shown in their itinerary. For this fictional domestic network, the recommended arrival time is two hours before departure, and the illustrative checked-bag cutoff is 45 minutes before departure. Airport-specific exceptions may apply in the synthetic scenario. Passengers should allow additional time when traveling with pets, children, or special equipment.

Baggage tags must match the passenger's current itinerary. The check-in agent may verify fare type, bag count, and item dimensions. A carry-on allowance does not excuse a passenger from following applicable security screening procedures. Staff should direct questions about prohibited articles to the relevant security authority rather than inventing permission based on a baggage allowance.
8.2 Gate-Side Baggage Review
At boarding, gate staff may inspect the size and number of cabin items. A bag exceeding the personal-item dimensions cannot be relabeled as a personal item simply to avoid a carry-on restriction. When overhead-bin capacity is exhausted, staff may gate check an eligible carry-on. The passenger should remove essential medications, travel documents, and fragile valuables before surrendering the bag.

If a passenger is on Basic Saver and has both a full-size carry-on and a personal item, the gate agent should first verify whether the reservation shows eligible status. If not, the passenger must follow the available checked-baggage or fare-change procedures. These steps support consistent application of Section 2.3 rather than creating a separate gate exception.
9. Customer Support and Policy Interpretation
9.1 Handling Conflicting Information
When a website summary, booking confirmation, and support message appear inconsistent, the current approved policy and the passenger's current fare details should be reviewed together. Staff should identify the exact rule and any exception rather than relying on a general statement such as “one bag is free.” The most specific applicable fare rule takes precedence over a broader allowance.

For RAG-based customer support, answers should be grounded in the retrieved policy text. A useful answer should state whether the requested action is permitted, identify conditions, and cite the relevant policy sections. The retrieval system may surface unrelated sections because they contain similar terms such as “bag,” “carry-on,” or “personal item.” Those sections should not override the actual governing rule.
9.2 Escalations and Exceptions
An agent should escalate an issue when a reservation shows conflicting baggage benefits, an accessibility accommodation requires review, or an item does not fit the standard categories. An escalation record should include the fare type, requested items, dimensions, relevant section numbers, and any existing approval. Staff should avoid promising exceptions that are not documented in the reservation or the approved policy.

A customer asking whether they can bring a carry-on bag and a backpack should normally receive an answer based on Sections 2.1, 2.2, and 2.3. Section 6 is relevant only if the customer also travels with an in-cabin pet. Other sections may provide background but should not distract from the controlling baggage rules.
Students should record the top three retrieved chunks when at least three chunks are available, their similarity scores if displayed, and whether each chunk is relevant, independently understandable, complete, or noisy. The highest similarity score does not by itself prove the best retrieval method. Students should compare whether the evidence supports the correct answer and whether the downstream language model would need to combine multiple retrieved sections.
